# understanding-waves — Context & Build Brief

An **interactive explainer environment** — in the same spirit as a "stock market explainer" but
about **ocean waves and tides**. The goal: let a curious person *play* with the ingredients that
shape waves and tides and genuinely understand how they work, through interactive simulation +
clear, friendly explanation.

This is **inspiration, not a rigid spec.** If you find a better way to make people understand and
feel wonder about waves, do that.

## The curiosity driving this (the questions to answer, beautifully)
- **Why is every wave unique — "there's never the same wave"?** (Great hook. The real answer is a
  wonderful teaching moment: the sea surface is the *superposition of countless waves* of different
  frequencies, directions and phases — so the exact shape never repeats. Show this literally.)
- **How are waves created?** What are *all the ingredients* that shape a wave?
- **Why is there always a stream/waves toward the land**, even as the tide retreats — why does it
  never just "flood" and stop? (Waves are *energy* travelling through water, often generated by
  wind far out at sea as swell, and they keep arriving regardless of the tide.)
- **What creates the tides?** Is it really the moon? (Yes — gravity of the Moon and Sun + Earth's
  rotation. Spring vs neap tides, etc.)
- **Waves vs tides** — the user intuitively blends them; a key job of this explainer is to gently
  *distinguish* them: **waves = wind-driven energy on the surface**; **tides = slow gravitational
  bulge from Moon & Sun.** Different causes, different timescales. (Consider covering both; the
  project could be "waves" with a strong tides section. Name is flexible — could become
  "understanding-tides" or cover both.)

## The "ingredients that shape waves" (build interactive controls around these)
- **Wind** — the main driver of surface waves: **wind speed, how long it blows (duration), and
  fetch** (the distance of open water it blows over). More of each → bigger waves.
- **Swell** — waves travelling far from where they were made; why calm-looking days still have sets.
- **Water depth & seabed (bathymetry)** — shoaling (waves slow, steepen and *break* in shallow
  water), refraction (waves bend toward the coast), why waves break near shore.
- **Coastline shape**, currents, storms.
- **Gravity & surface tension** — restoring forces; capillary (ripples) vs gravity waves.
- **Moon & Sun** — tides, which raise/lower the whole water level and modulate everything.

## Suggested form (keep it clean, open-source)
- A beautiful **real-time wave simulation** in the browser (canvas/WebGL/SVG). The superposition of
  many sine waves is ideal — it both looks like the sea *and* demonstrates "no two waves alike."
- **Interactive controls / sliders**: wind speed, duration, fetch, depth → watch wave height, length
  and breaking change live, with a plain-language readout of what's happening and why.
- A **tide module**: Moon (and Sun) position → the tidal bulge, spring vs neap tides, the daily
  rhythm — ideally an animation you can scrub.
- **Explanatory panels / scrollytelling**: short, friendly text that appears as you explore, like a
  guided tour of each ingredient. Make people go "ohh, that's why."
- Optionally use **fal.ai** (key `FAL_KEY` in `~/knowledge/.env`) for hero imagery, but the core
  should be an interactive simulation, not static pictures.
- **No authentication** — public demo (we run inside a private VPN container); fully click-through.

## Build approach
- **Start building a prototype early** — get a living, interactive wave on screen, then layer in the
  ingredients and the tides module.
- Prioritise the feeling of *understanding + wonder*. Accurate enough to teach, simple enough to feel.
